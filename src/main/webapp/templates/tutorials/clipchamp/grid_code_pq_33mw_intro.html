Network

33 MW farm at the 110 kV POC

Same template as the park-controller video. Two 30 kV collector strings feed a 40 MVA 110/30 kV substation. A 5 km 110 kV cable reaches the grid-operator bus. A shunt reactor sits farm-side of the POC.

Plant (ours to control)

  • 10 × 3.3 MVA WTGs at 0.69 kV
  • 10 × 3.75 MVA 30/0.65 kV padmounts
  • Two 30 kV strings, 5 turbines each
  • 40 MVA 110/30 kV main transformer
  • Shunt reactor at substation 110 kV

Grid interface

  • POC = Point of connection — 110 kV
  • 5 km 110 kV cable to the POC
  • Park Controller already lists all 10 WTGs
  • First P-Q run: tap and shunt control off
  • Everything grid-side of the POC is TSO

Connection-point P-Q

Plot P at the PoC, not generator P

The sweep still dispatches active power on the turbines. The chart uses net P and Q from each solved load flow at the PCC. Grid-code Pmax is that delivered P, not nameplate.

Sweep / generator P

  • Set as % of Pn on the selected WTGs
  • Pn = 0 uses the sum of ratings
  • Shows up in the CSV as P dispatch
  • Not the Y-axis of the red envelope

Net P at the POC

  • After collector and transformer losses
  • Typically a little below installed P
  • Red Y-axis = Net P at PCC
  • Blue scaled to that same Pmax

Plant Q dispatch

Local Q versus Park Controller

Same farm and same grid-code template. Only the way plant Q is set changes. Use park dispatch when you want the operating picture at the POC.

Local Q on each WTG

  • Each unit Qmax / Qmin from its P–Q curve
  • Park controllers taken out of service
  • Machine-limit envelope
  • Same idea as P-Q & U-Q plant Q

Park — constant Q at PoC

  • BinarySearchControl to a POC Q setpoint
  • Q reduced if loading or voltage binds
  • Matches how the farm is operated
  • First run in this tutorial
