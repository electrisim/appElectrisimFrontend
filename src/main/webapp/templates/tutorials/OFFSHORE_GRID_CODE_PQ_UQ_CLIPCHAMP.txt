Grid Code Compliance P Q and U Q, 450 megawatt offshore wind farm, Electrisim

Today we check grid code compliance of a 450 megawatt offshore wind farm in open-source Electrisim. We run two studies at the 400 kilovolt point of connection. The P Q study maps reactive power capability across the whole active power range. The U Q study holds the plant at maximum active power and sweeps the grid voltage. Both compare the plant against the European network code requirements for generators.

Six steps. Open the offshore template. Walk through the plant. See how the controllers share reactive power. Run the P Q study. Read the results, including the reactive power of each element and an overload check. Then run the U Q study and export a report image.

Step one. Open Electrisim in the browser. Go to File, New, and choose offshore wind farm, revision 7. The model opens with all equipment, controllers, and settings already on the diagram.

Step two, the plant. Thirty wind turbines of 15 megawatts each, 450 megawatts in total, on a 66 kilovolt inter-array network. Two offshore substations step up to 275 kilovolts with three-winding transformers. 275 kilovolt export cables bring the power onshore. The onshore substation has a three-winding transformer with an on-load tap changer, a variable shunt reactor, and a STATCOM rated plus and minus 200 megavar on the 275 kilovolt busbar. The point of connection is the 400 kilovolt busbar of the transmission system operator. Long cables generate a lot of reactive power, so shunt reactors absorb it, and the STATCOM fine-tunes the result.

Step three, the controllers. A good design avoids controllers that fight each other, so each one has its own job. The tap changer of the onshore transformer regulates its voltage. The variable shunt reactor follows the active power flowing in the export cable: when the farm is idle, the cable is mostly capacitive, so the reactor absorbs more. The Park Controller dispatches the wind turbines to hold constant reactive power at the 275 kilovolt offshore busbar, here minus 150 megavar. Finally, the Plant Supervisory Controller makes the final correction at the 400 kilovolt point of connection. It follows a reactive power versus active power characteristic taken from the grid code template. The shunt reactors take the coarse part in steps, and the STATCOM supplies the exact remainder, so the result at the busbar is smooth. The reactors only switch when the STATCOM leaves its reserve band, so it always keeps headroom in both directions.

Step four, the P Q study. Go to Simulate, then Grid Code Compliance, P Q. Select the point of connection busbar and the external grid. Tick the thirty wind turbines. For plant reactive power dispatch, choose Park Controller as configured, select the Park Controller, and select the Plant Supervisory Controller. In this mode the controllers run exactly as on the diagram, and the STATCOM is pushed to its capacitive and inductive rating to draw the two edges of the capability. Sweep active power from 0 to 100 percent in steps of 10 percent. Enter the voltage levels at the point of connection, for example 0.9, 0.95, 1.0 and 1.05 per unit. Tip: start with 1.0 per unit only. Four voltage levels mean hundreds of load flows and take a few minutes. Tick three-winding transformer tap control and shunt reactor control. As the grid code requirement, choose the European network code inner envelope for power park modules. Then click Calculate. A progress window shows each voltage level, and you can stop the run between load flows.

Step five, read the results. The window opens maximized. The red area is the plant capability: net active and reactive power at the point of connection, after all cable and transformer losses. So the top of the red area is below 450 megawatts. The blue area is the grid code requirement, scaled to the maximum active power that really reaches the point of connection. The plant is compliant when the red area fully covers the blue area at every voltage. Inductive reactive power is on the left, capacitive on the right.

Click any red point to show that load flow on the single line diagram. Every busbar, cable, and transformer shows its result for exactly that operating point.

Now turn on Element Q. Each group of devices is drawn along both edges. Diamonds and solid lines show the capacitive edge, triangles and dashed lines show the inductive edge. You can see the wind turbines, the STATCOM at its rating, and the shunt reactors at their extreme steps. One lesson from this chart: on the capacitive edge the Plant Supervisory Controller switches its reactors to the step that absorbs least. If a reactor can be switched off, its reactive power drops to zero there. If in reality that reactor is fixed and cannot be switched, remove it from the Plant Supervisory Controller, because otherwise the capacitive capability looks larger than it really is.

Next, click Check overloads. It reads the load flow already stored with every point, so it adds no simulation time. It lists every line and transformer above the loading limit, with its worst case and the number of cases above the limit. Click Show on diagram to open that case. Here it points to the onshore transformer at the inductive edge and low voltage, which is useful input for the transformer rating.

Step six, the U Q study. Go to Simulate, then Grid Code Compliance, U Q. The settings are the same as in the P Q study. Again choose Park Controller as configured with the Plant Supervisory Controller. Active power is held at 100 percent of rated power, and the voltage at the point of connection is swept, here from 0.9 to 1.1 per unit. As the requirement, choose the European network code U Q over P max template. Click Calculate.

The red curves show the highest and lowest reactive power the plant can deliver at each voltage. The blue box is the required range. In our run, the plant reaches more than 330 megavar capacitive and more than 270 megavar inductive at every voltage, so it covers the requirement of about 148 megavar in both directions. Element Q and Check overloads work here too, exactly as in the P Q study.

Finally, the report. Click Copy image to put a report-ready chart on the clipboard, with title, grid code template, voltage, compliance verdict and full legend, ready to paste into a document or a LinkedIn article. Save image downloads the same picture as a file, and Download CSV exports all points, element contributions and overloads.

That is grid code compliance at the point of connection for a 450 megawatt offshore wind farm, from one model, in the browser. The model is free to download from electrisim dot com, and the documentation explains every setting. Thanks for watching.
