Data centre fault ride-through, STATCOM and U P S, Electrisim

Today we check whether a data centre rides through a fault in the grid, in open-source Electrisim. Large data centres draw their power through U P S rectifiers. When the voltage dips, the U P S moves the servers to its battery, and the grid suddenly loses hundreds of megawatts. Grid operators now ask large loads to ride through: stay connected, and get their power back quickly. We will size a STATCOM for the reactive current a dip asks for, and simulate the U P S in the time domain.

Six steps. Open the template. Look at the data centre and its U P S. Look at the STATCOM. Size the STATCOM for fault ride-through. Simulate a remote fault, with and without the STATCOM. And finally a close-in fault, where we tune the U P S until the data centre passes.

Step one. Open Electrisim in the browser, go to File, New, and search for data centre. The template is in the Tutorials group: data centre fault ride-through. Click Create. A 138 kilovolt utility grid with 8000 M V A of short-circuit power feeds the point of interconnection over two lines. A 400 M V A transformer steps down to the 34.5 kilovolt campus bus, with the 300 megawatt data centre and a STATCOM rated plus and minus 150 megavar.

Step two, the data centre. Double-click the load and open the Data center tab. The computational load model is switched on. 85 percent of the load is I T equipment behind the U P S, and the rest is cooling. Below 0.85 per unit the U P S moves the I T load to its battery. It goes back to the grid once the voltage has been above 0.9 per unit for one second, and then walks the load back in over five seconds. The requirement says: after the voltage has recovered, the power must be back at 90 percent within one second. And this is the ride-through curve: zero volts for 150 milliseconds, then 0.7, 0.85 and 0.9 per unit. As long as the voltage stays above this curve, the data centre must ride through.

Step three, the STATCOM. On its Dynamics tab, the STATCOM is a converter without active power. It regulates the bus voltage, and in a dip it adds reactive current: a k factor of 2 times the voltage drop beyond a 10 percent deadband, up to its current limit. Run a load flow. The STATCOM already supplies about 63 megavar for the data centre and holds the campus bus at 1.0 per unit.

Step four, size the STATCOM. Go to Simulate, Grid-Forming STATCOM Sizing. Select the STATCOM, choose the data centre load as the facility, and the requirement set for a data centre. Its fault ride-through duty asks for additional reactive current, k times the voltage drop, on the rated current of the facility. For the deepest dip, to zero, that is limited to one per unit. Click Calculate. The data centre needs no supercapacitors for this, but the converter must carry 306 M V A of reactive current, about 5 kilo amps. Our 150 M V A STATCOM is too small for that requirement: it covers dips down to about 0.86 per unit. That is the size to discuss with the grid operator.

Step five, the time domain. Go to Simulate, Transient Stability. Set 60 hertz and 10 seconds. Put a fault on the 138 kilovolt utility bus from one second to 1.15 seconds, through a reactance of 0.1 per unit, like a fault further away in the grid. Choose the point of interconnection as the P O I bus, and run. The verdict is a pass. The campus voltage dips to about 0.88 per unit, above the U P S transfer voltage, so the U P S stays on the grid and the I T load never leaves it.

Now take the STATCOM out of service and run the same fault again. Without it, the campus voltage starts lower, at 0.97 per unit, and dips to 0.84. That is just below the transfer voltage. The U P S moves 257 megawatts to its battery, waits one second, and walks the load back in over five seconds. The power is back 5.5 seconds after the voltage. The verdict is fail: power back too late. So even a 150 megavar STATCOM keeps this data centre on the grid in a remote fault.

Step six, a close-in fault. Put the STATCOM back in service, and move the fault to the point of interconnection, as a bolted fault. Now the voltage is zero for 150 milliseconds. That is inside the ride-through curve, but no load can draw power at zero volts, so every U P S transfers. With the default settings the power comes back 5.5 seconds after the voltage. Fail. The STATCOM gives its full reactive current in the dip and regulates the voltage when the load drops.

The fix is in the U P S. Open the data centre load again, and set the return delay to 0.2 seconds and the walk-in to half a second. A shorter return delay brings the U P S back to the grid sooner, and a short walk-in ramps the servers up quickly. Check with the U P S supplier that its rectifier allows this. Run the same fault. The power is back 0.7 seconds after the voltage, and the verdict is now a pass. The chart shows the power the data centre draws from the grid, and Export C S V saves every curve for your report.

That is fault ride-through of a data centre in Electrisim: a STATCOM sized for the k factor reactive current, and the U P S simulated in the time domain, with a clear verdict. The template is free in File, New, and the documentation explains every setting. Thanks for watching.
